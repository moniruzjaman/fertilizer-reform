05 — ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

Three structural dysfunctions sustain the crisis.

None of them are acts of nature. All of them are design choices — and all of them can be redesigned.

Collusion & Diversion

The Additional Secretary for Fertiliser Management has acknowledged that "negligence and collusion among some dealers and field-level officials" divert subsidized fertilizer to unauthorized markets.

Regulatory Erosion

The 2009 dealer framework has become obsolete, with sub-dealers and retailers operating with minimal accountability — more hands in the chain, more points of leakage.

Information Asymmetry

Farmers lack credible, site-specific advice and rely on dealers with a vested interest in over-selling — "more is better" persists because no one tells them otherwise.

07 — REGIONAL PROOF

Our neighbours have already run this experiment.

India proves biometric distribution works at scale. Vietnam proves less fertilizer can be more. China proves digital infrastructure changes behaviour. The Philippines warns what happens without tracking.

Case 01 · India — DBT Biometric Revolution

Subsidy released only after the verified sale

Since 2018, Aadhaar-enabled PoS devices authenticate farmers at retail shops; subsidies reach companies only after actual sales, on a "no denial" basis. Now piloting land- and crop-based pre-booking limits.

2.87 lakhRetailers
8.4 croreFarmers served
50 bagsMonthly ceiling / buyer
Lesson for BangladeshBiometric authentication at point of sale is non-negotiable for eliminating diversion; land-linked quotas are the next frontier.
Case 02 · Vietnam — Precision & Sustainability

"One Must, Five Reductions" and the Mekong Delta programme

Certified seeds mandated while reducing seeding rates, fertilizer, pesticides, water and post-harvest losses. The 1 Million Hectare High-Quality, Low-Emission Rice Program delivered remarkable results.

40%Cut in chemical N
31.3%Avg fertilizer reduction
20%N + irrigation water cut
Lesson for BangladeshReducing chemical dependency through precision agriculture improves farmer economics and environmental resilience together.
Case 03 · China — Zero Growth & Digital Agriculture

Digital backbone before subsidy reform

The Action Plan for Zero Growth in Chemical Fertilizer Use targeted utilization >40% and soil-testing coverage >90%. Over 99% of administrative villages have fiber + 4G; internet-informed farmers proved 3.5% more fertilizer-efficient.

>90%Soil testing coverage

08 — REFORM ARCHITECTURE

A four-layer system, plus eight requirements for accountability.

The core recommendation: transition from a blanket, dealer-centric subsidy model to a biometrically authenticated, land-linked, digitally tracked distribution system — the right farmer, the right input, the right price, the right time.

Layer 1 · Identity & Land

Krishak Card + NID + BDS/EDLMS

Biometric linkage to verified land parcels and crop declarations.

Layer 2 · Point of Sale

Biometric PoS devices

Aadhaar-like authentication at dealer shops; offline capability with local server sync.

Layer 3 · Tracking & Transparency

Central digital ledger + blockchain

Immutable record from import/production → dealer → farmer; public dashboard.

Layer 4 · Advisory & Verification

Digital agri platform + satellite NDVI

Mobile-first soil health recommendations; satellite verification of crop declarations.

6.1 Hybrid Edge-Cloud Architecture

Given connectivity gaps in rural Bangladesh, local servers at union/upazila level process transactions offline and sync with central servers when connectivity resumes — ensuring inclusion of remote and climate-vulnerable regions. No farmer excluded.

6.2 Soil Testing & Formula Fertilization (STFFT)

A nationwide Soil Health Card programme — mirroring China's STFFT — provides localized, crop-specific recommendations, shifting farmer behaviour from "more is better" to "precision is profitable."

01

Biometric Authentication at PoS

NID + Krishak Card as authentication key, mirroring India's Aadhaar-enabled PoS model.

02

Land-Linked Farmer Identification

Quotas tied to verified land area and crop type using BDS/EDLMS geospatial data — stops wealthy capture.

03

Real-Time Tracking & Transparency

Every transaction — import to farmer purchase — recorded centrally. Blockchain option for immutable audit trail.

04

Dealer Accountability & Penalties

Swift penalties for dealers who fail to lift stock, divert to black market, or sell above official prices.

05

Crop Declaration + Satellite Verification

Pre-season crop declaration via mobile app or Union Digital Centers, cross-verified with NDVI satellite imagery.

06

Phased Transition from Blanket Subsidies

Expand soil testing, strengthen advisory services, roll out e-vouchers to reach poor and climate-vulnerable farmers.

07

Local Server Architecture

Hybrid edge-cloud with union/upazila-level offline servers, syncing when connectivity returns.

08

Independent Oversight Body

Dedicated oversight with farmer, civil society and expert representation — publishing regular public reports.

14 — THE PATH OF BIR UTTAM PRESIDENT ZIAUR RAHMAN

A warehouse full of fertilizer means nothing if the farmer's hands remain empty.

From the 1977 canal-digging programme and the restructuring of BADC to the 2026 digital land-linked quotas — the same mandate, renewed.

Bir Uttam President Ziaur Rahman, the Father of Bangladeshi Nationalism, did not merely seek political sovereignty; he sought economic dignity for the farmer — the Krishak — whom he recognized as the backbone of the nation. His vision: a disciplined, self-reliant Bangladesh where rural prosperity is not charity, but institutional justice and administrative integrity.

To walk his path today is to declare that the syndicate, the colluding dealer, and the negligent official are enemies of the state no less than any external threat. It is to insist that Bangladesh's sovereignty is incomplete while its food system remains captive to black markets and global price shocks.

"As President Ziaur Rahman trusted the courage and capacity of the Bangladeshi people to forge their destiny, so too must this Ministry trust the Bangladeshi farmer with the tools of modernity — digital identity, scientific knowledge, and fair markets — and then get out of his way."

Discipline in Governance

The biometric PoS, the blockchain ledger and the public dashboard are not merely technologies — they are instruments of institutional discipline. The officer, the dealer and the minister are all accountable to the farmer.

Empowerment, Not Dependency

Soil-tested, land-linked entitlements treat the farmer not as a passive recipient of state charity, but as a rational economic actor stewarding the land with precision and pride.

Self-Reliance

Reviving domestic production, reducing import dependence, and leveraging Bangladesh's own digital infrastructure (BDS, Krishak Card, NID) builds a system that is resilient, sovereign, and future-proof.
